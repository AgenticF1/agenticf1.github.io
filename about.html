About

Who writes this, and why

AgenticF1 is a personal project. A little background still helps, so you know what the analysis is good for and where it is thin.

Section 01

Who writes this

I hold a PhD in computer science and follow Formula 1 closely. My research was in 3D modeling, agents, planning, and a little physics simulation. My thesis title? You guess.

What that means for the analysis: the part I am confident in is the modeling. Going from raw data, to a model, to checking whether the model is really saying something, to wiring it into an agentic system that does most of the work with me in the loop. That is the background these posts lean on.

Where I am weak: I have no serious mechanical or aerodynamics background, so I have little to say about why a car is built the way it is. When a result turns on that, I say so rather than guess.

No team affiliation and no inside information. Everything here comes from public data. I am fairly neutral on the entire field.

Section 02

Why I build this

When I watch a race I spend as much time on the onboards and the detailed timing data as on the broadcast. I have been told this is an ADHD trait. It might be.

Mostly I come away with questions. Why did one team split their runs like that and another pair their cars? What if the safety car had come three laps later? How fast was this driver actually going, once you take out the strategy and the tyres?

The community has done the hard work of opening up data that can answer some of this. AgenticF1 is me using it on my own questions, and sharing the answers that turned out to be interesting.

Section 03

The pipeline and the model

AgenticF1 is a set of pipelines that model and analyze qualifying and race pace. On top of the shared model, some weekends get purpose-built tools for a specific race or situation, so the analysis can be more complete and more targeted.

The posts are the best way to see what I have actually built.
